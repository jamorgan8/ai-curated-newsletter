Federal Politics

Supreme Court Delivers Key Rulings on Voter Eligibility and Redistricting

The Supreme Court issued two significant rulings on Friday. In a temporary victory for the Trump administration, the Court lifted restrictions on a federal voter eligibility database, allowing states to utilize the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) program. This program is intended to verify the eligibility of voters, a move supported by the administration's campaign against noncitizen voting, while critics express concerns it could lead to eligible voters being wrongly removed from rolls. The Court's three liberal justices dissented from this emergency order.

Separately, the Supreme Court rejected an attempt to implement a new congressional map in Missouri, which was backed by President Donald Trump. This decision effectively ends a protracted legal dispute, ensuring that Missouri residents will vote under the same congressional districts used in the 2022 and 2024 elections for the upcoming November midterms. The justices let stand a Missouri Supreme Court ruling that mandated voter approval via a November referendum for the new map to be used.
