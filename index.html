Nashville Selected for Bloomberg Housing Initiative

Nashville has been chosen as one of the initial 15 U.S. cities to participate in Bloomberg Philanthropies' new housing initiative. The city is set to receive between $450,000 and $750,000 to implement technology, engage architects for permit-ready plans, and hire staff to accelerate housing supply growth through permit reform. This initiative aims to address Nashville's projected need for approximately 90,000 new housing units over the next decade.

Anthropic Proposes Federal AI Safety Bill to U.S. Lawmakers

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, has reportedly circulated a draft bill among U.S. lawmakers aimed at establishing federal regulation for artificial intelligence. The proposal seeks to address the current lack of federal legislation concerning the risks posed by advanced AI systems. According to reports, the draft bill suggests that the government could intervene to block a model's release or use if it presents an imminent risk of causing over $1 billion in damages or seriously injuring or killing more than 50 people, contingent on judicial approval. A company spokesperson indicated that the proposal empowers the federal government to deter dangerous deployments of powerful models and mandates developers to conduct testing, maintain transparency, undergo independent evaluations, and implement robust security programs. This move comes as industry leaders, including Anthropic's co-founder Jack Clark, emphasize the urgent need for guardrails to manage AI's rapid advancements.

China Announces New Restrictions on AI and Tech Exports

China has unveiled plans to restrict or ban the export of 40 technologies, including various artificial intelligence components, chips, and solar power innovations. This move, announced by the Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology, is presented as a "necessary" measure to safeguard national security. The draft document outlines prohibitions on exporting core AI large model technologies trained with sensitive national data and AI agents designed for critical environments like national defense. Exports of high-parameter AI models (2 trillion or more parameters) or those with world-leading capabilities could also face restrictions. This development comes amidst ongoing technology competition with countries like the United States, even as the two nations have engaged in dialogues concerning AI safety and governance.

Google DeepMind Grants AlphaFold 3 Access and $3M Compute to European Scientists

Google DeepMind, in collaboration with Google Cloud and the European Commission's RAISE program, has committed $3 million in compute resources and targeted access to advanced AI tools for up to 200 European researchers. This initiative aims to accelerate biological and environmental science by placing AlphaFold 3, an AI model that predicts protein structures and molecular interactions, directly into the hands of working scientists. AlphaFold 3 is expected to transform months of lab-based trials into rapid digital simulations by predicting how proteins interact within broader biological systems. This substantial compute backing is designed to overcome hardware limitations that have historically hindered non-commercial labs from scaling top-tier AI models. The program also includes access to other science AI models such as WeatherNext, AlphaGenome, and AlphaEvolve.

Meta to Train AI Models Using Linked Business Websites from October 30

Meta is updating its commercial terms of service, effective October 30, 2026, which will require business accounts to permit the company to train its AI models on content from linked websites, not just posts directly on Meta platforms. This policy change authorizes Meta to utilize automated software, such as web scrapers and crawlers, to access, index, analyze, and collect data from domains, webpages, or URLs associated with business products or services. Meta may also create "test credentials" to bypass access controls for this purpose. The company's updated terms explicitly state that content from these domains may be used to "provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity" of Meta's products and services. This update has implications for businesses using Meta platforms, as there will be no opt-out option for this data scraping. This has direct consumer impact for businesses and their website content linked to Meta products.
